Douglas Miller's webpage

Welcome to my webpage! I am a fourth-year undergraduate student-researcher studying physics and math at Florida Atlantic University. Here you will find a description of my research, some of my writing, and my CV . As for my interests, I am broadly interested in condensed matter physics (CMP) theory and quantum gravity. In CMP theory, I am most interested in topological quantum matter and phases, quantum Hall interferometry, and anyonic physics. In quantum gravity, I am most interested in the spinfoam approach. I also have academic interests in HEP theory, and appreciate the use of geometrical methods - and diverse mathematics - in physics, particularly in physics that has experimental relavance.

Research

Here are brief descriptions of two of my current research projects (and an independent "research exploration"), along with PDFs (to be written) which explain them in more detail.

CMP Theory

  • Scattering Theory for Quantum Hall Interferometry During the Summer 2026 University of Rochester NSF REU, with supervision of Dr. Chaitanya Murthy, I developed a gauge-independent treatment of electron scattering near quantum point-contacts for quantum Hall interferometry.
  • Path Integral Derivation of Aharonov-Bohm This is more of an independent research project than a formal research program. I am investigating the Aharonov-Bohm effect via the path integral approach to quantum mechanics. In particular, I am investigating the caustics in the path integral for the effect. This has, suprisingly, not been done before, and the derivations that do exist rely on (reasonable) heuristics.

Quantum Gravity

  • Ponzano-Regge spinfoam model With Dr. Jonathan Engle at Florida Atlantic University, I am researching a subtle tension in the equations of motion for the Ponzano-Regge spinfoam model. During this research, I have developed code to compute spinfoam amplitudes and expectation values of geometrical operators for the Ponzano-Regge model.

Physics

  • Path Integral Derivation of the Aharonov-Bohm Effect These notes explain the standard derivation, though somewhat difficult to find detailed discussions of, the Aharonov-Bohm effect using the path-integral approach to quantum mechanics.
  • Classical Mechanics and Symplectic Structure These notes summarize the three main formulations of classical mechanics, Newtonian, Lagrangian, and Hamiltonian, with emphasis on mathematical precision in the discussion of Lagrangian mechanics. Also discussed is the symplectic structure underlying the Hamiltonian formualation.
