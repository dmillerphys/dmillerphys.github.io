Welcome to my webpage! I am a fourth-year undergraduate student-researcher studying physics and math at Florida Atlantic University. Here you will find a description of my research, some of my writing, and my CV. I am interested in and have research experience in condensed matter physics (CMP) theory and quantum gravity. In CMP theory, I am most interested in topological quantum matter and phases, quantum Hall interferometry, and anyonic physics. In quantum gravity, I am most interested in the spinfoam approach. I am broadly interested in the use of diverse mathematics in describing novel phenomena, whether it be at the atomic scales or at the singularities of black holes.

Research

Here are brief descriptions of two of my current research projects, along with PDFs which explain them in more detail (the Quantum Gravity PDF is to be written).

CMP Theory

  • Electron Scattering Theory for Quantum Hall Interferometry During the Summer 2026 University of Rochester NSF REU, where I was supervised by Prof. Chaitanya Murthy, I developed a gauge-independent treatment of electron scattering near quantum point-contacts for quantum Hall interferometry. This project was motivated by the 2020 experiment reported in Direct Observation of Anyonic Braiding Statistics, where the Manfra group at Purdue University detected anyonic particle statistics in a quantum Hall interferometer. We want to understand how implementing a quantum dot in the scattering region of the interferometer arXiv2403.12139v2 can measure the anyonic exchange phase \( e^{i \theta} \), and not twice the exchange phase \(e^{2 i \theta}\).

Quantum Gravity

  • Non-Zero Levi-Civita Curvature in Ponzano-Regge At Florida Atlantic University, where I am advised by Prof. Jonathan Engle, I am researching a subtle tension between the equations of motion in two different representations of the Ponzano-Regge spinfoam model of 3D quantum gravity. In the group variable formulation, the equations of motion are flatness, as expected for a quantum theory of 3D gravity. However, in the semi-classical limit of the spin formulation, the equations of motion are not flatness, but rather introduce locall orientations, giving non-flatness for some choices of orientation. During this research, I have developed code to compute spinfoam amplitudes and expectation values of geometrical operators defined in the model. A manuscript, to be published with Prof. Jonathan Engle, is in writing.

Writing

Here is some expository writing.

Physics

  • Path Integral Derivation of the Aharonov-Bohm Effect These notes explain the standard derivation of the Aharonov-Bohm effect using the path-integral approach to quantum mechanics. The use of path-integral methods to derive the Aharonov-Bohm effect is not very commonplace in literature, and most derivations use a few heuristics in important places. These notes attempt to make the derivation more mathematically precise.
  • Classical Mechanics and Symplectic Structure These notes summarize the three main formulations of classical mechanics, Newtonian, Lagrangian, and Hamiltonian, with emphasis on mathematical precision in the discussion of Lagrangian mechanics. Also discussed is the symplectic structure underlying the Hamiltonian formualation.
